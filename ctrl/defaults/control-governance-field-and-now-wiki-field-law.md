# One wiki per register, returns between them

**Status:** distributed default — becomes human source on adoption

My world keeps one wiki at each register — the root wiki at `Control/agents/wiki/wiki.json` for cross-project and personal cognition, and one project wiki at `ProjectCentral/agents/wiki/wiki.json` for each project's own cognition. Two registers, no third field. Work that spans projects records where it was worked and returns through the field of its register, not through a wiki that happens to be nearest.

A wiki is agent-maintained knowledge, never source. You do not edit `wiki.json` directly, at either register. What a session learned that deserves wiki standing travels as a return: NOW promotion writes the returned source under `agents/wiki/returns/**` with its lineage already stamped, and the wiki's own maintenance — incorporation, refraction, cleanup — stays with the wiki owner's procedure (`aikit wiki ...`). The return is the only door.

A project's documents — its README, its docs tree, its AGENTS file — are that project's ground inside its repository, under its conventions and its authorship path. Work on them happens in that repository, to that repository's standard. Central's wiki holds knowledge about that work; it does not hold the work. When you move between a project's docs and Central's field, you are moving between registers: record in the project's field what belongs to the project, in the root field what belongs to the world, and let the wikis learn through returns, not through copies.

The root wiki does not aggregate the project wikis. Cross-project resonance is real knowledge and it lives at the root with its own provenance; a project's self-knowledge is not lifted out of its project to make the root wiki look complete. Each wiki discloses its register; neither pretends to be the other.

Cleaning the field is part of keeping it: stale edges, dangling refs and dead learnings are the wiki owner's maintenance to run, from evidence, with provenance — not a session's side errand and not silent deletion. The continuity programme owns how wikis ride sessions and hooks; this law owns where knowledge belongs while it rides.
